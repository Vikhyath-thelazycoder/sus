# P3 evidence: Retrieval and evaluation

Date: 2026-11-06 · Data: Cloudflare production D1 `sustainiq-db` + Vectorize `sustainiq-chunks` (20 papers, 881 chunks) · Code: `src/worker/rag/search.ts`, `scripts/eval.ts`, `data/golden.json`

## T-3.1 Golden set

`data/golden.json`: **35 answerable + 5 unanswerable** questions. The plan asked for 30 + 5; the extra five give every paper at least one question. Each answerable question gives the paper (DOI), section, page range and a short gold answer. Each question was written from chunk text pulled from production D1, so pages and sections match what is stored. Styles: 22 paraphrase (different words from the paper), 10 exact (a named term or number), 1 vague, 2 with typos. The unanswerable ones are nearby topics the corpus does not cover (perovskite cells, EU reuse targets, battery recycling, spider silk) and one off-topic question.

**Scoring rule:** a retrieved chunk is correct when it comes from the gold paper and section and its pages overlap the gold pages. Matching on paper, section and pages, not chunk ID, keeps the set valid if the chunks are re-made (T-3.7).

**Limits, stated plainly:**
- The questions were written by the builder from the stored chunks, not taken from real users. This makes recall optimistic, because the words are close to the source.
- A sibling chunk on the same page and section also counts as correct.
- **Owner review of the set: pending.**

## T-3.2 to T-3.5 Pipeline

`retrieve(env, query, ownerId)`:

1. Two searches run in parallel:
   - **Meaning search:** `bge-m3` query embedding, Vectorize top 30, with the filter `{ owner_id: { $in: ["public", ownerId] } }` built only on the server (`ownerFilter`).
   - **Keyword search:** FTS5 BM25 top 30. The query becomes quoted literal terms (max 32 terms, 64 characters each) and runs as a bound parameter, scoped to `owner_id IN ('public', ?)` on the joined `chunks` row. If FTS5 throws, the search logs a warning and returns no results.
2. **RRF** (k = 60) merges both lists and keeps the top 10.
3. **Owner re-check in D1:** a chunk survives only if D1 says it is `public` or the caller's own, its paper has the same owner, and the paper's status is `ready`.
4. **Rerank:** `bge-reranker-base` scores the 10 candidates. The final order is the RRF of the hybrid order and the reranker order (see T-3.7). The top 6 are kept, and each keeps its raw `rerankScore`.
5. **Passages:** each hit gets the chunk before and after it from the same section, as long as the total stays within 3,000 tokens. Touching chunks merge into one passage, and the ~15% sentence overlap is removed when they are joined. Passages are ordered best-first and numbered `n` for citations in P4.

Admin-only probe for the eval: `POST /api/admin/search` (strict schema, admin token, searches public papers only, returns IDs and pages, no text).

Tests (`test/rag.test.ts`, 12 tests, Workers runtime with real D1/FTS5):
- **Owner isolation (SEC §13 #4):** B's `secret-B-token` chunk is never returned to A through FTS. It is also never returned through Vectorize, even when a fake index **ignores the filter** and returns B's vector first: the D1 re-check drops it.
- **Injection (SEC §13 #5, #6):** SQL and FTS5 payloads (`' OR 1=1 --`, `owner_id:*`, `NEAR(`, `"`, `*`, `^`, `PLA) OR (secret`) cause no error and no leak.
- **Exact terms:** PLA, PBAT and ASTM D6400 are found.
- **RRF:** the guide's worked example (`[D,A,C] + [A,B,D] → [A,D,B,C]`) gives the expected order.
- **Passages:** best-first order, same-section neighbours only, merge with overlap removal, 3,000-token cap.

## T-3.7 Tuning

All settings were measured offline. Each question's vector top 50, keyword top 50 and reranker scores were fetched once, then every setting was replayed over that cache. Results on 35 answerable questions:

| Final order | searchK | fuseTop | r@1 | r@3 | r@5 | r@6 | MRR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hybrid | 10 | any | 0.83 | 0.94 | 0.94 | 0.97 | 0.89 |
| hybrid | 20–50 | any | 0.83 | 0.97 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.90 |
| reranker only | 30 | 10 / 20 | 0.77 / 0.74 | 0.91 / 0.94 | 0.97 | 0.97 | 0.85 |
| **RRF(hybrid, reranker)** | 20–50 | **10** | **0.91** | 0.97 | **1.00** | 1.00 | **0.94** |
| RRF(hybrid, reranker) | 20–50 | 20 / 30 | 0.89 | 0.97 | 0.97 | 1.00 | 0.93 |

**Decisions and the numbers behind them:**
- **The reranker is not used alone.** On its own, `bge-reranker-base` (the only reranker on Workers AI) is worse than plain hybrid (MRR 0.85 vs 0.90). It sometimes ranks a topically close chunk from another paper first. For q04, about TEMPO oxidation drawbacks, it put an aerogel paper first. Its scores bunch up near 1.0 (14 of 35 above 0.99). Fusing its order with the hybrid order corrects most of these mistakes. This matches the RAG guide's caveat (§5.4): "test on your data".
- **fuseTop 20 → 10.** It scores better (r@1 0.91 vs 0.89) and halves the reranker's input; measured p50 went from 1,204 ms to 986 ms.
- **searchK stays 30.** 20, 30 and 50 give the same results, and 10 loses one question at r@6. 30 leaves room for a larger corpus.
- **Keep the top 6.** r@5 is already 1.00, and 6 leaves one spare. The six hits average about 2,077 tokens; neighbour expansion fills the rest of the 3,000-token budget.
- **Chunk size stays at ~350 tokens.** With recall@6 = 1.00 and recall@10 = 1.00, chunking is not the bottleneck on this set. Re-chunking means re-embedding all 881 chunks with nothing measured to gain. Revisit when the golden set has real user questions.

**Score histograms (RAG §5.7).** No threshold has been set yet; P4 decides how to decline.

| Top score | answerable (35) | unanswerable (5) |
|---|---|---|
| reranker 0.00–0.10 | 1 | 4 |
| reranker 0.10–0.50 | 1 | 1 |
| reranker 0.50–0.90 | 7 | 0 |
| reranker 0.90–1.00 | 26 | 0 |
| cosine 0.30–0.50 | 0 | 3 |
| cosine 0.50–0.60 | 6 | 2 |
| cosine 0.60–0.80 | 29 | 0 |

(Pool = hybrid top 20. With the tuned top 10, the unanswerable maximum drops from 0.374 to 0.068.)

- The reranker score separates the two groups better than cosine: cosine overlaps between 0.50 and 0.60.
- Even so, in the tuned run 3 answerable questions score under 0.30, two of them with the right chunk ranked first:
  - q33 ("what holds back light-driven redox catalysis"): 0.038, correct chunk ranked first
  - q01: 0.196
  - q35: 0.281, correct chunk ranked first
- A hard cut-off would silently refuse them. P4 should treat a low score as a signal for the model's "I couldn't find this" answer, not as a hard refusal.
- With only 5 unanswerable questions, the sample is too small to set a threshold.

## Data finding (from P2)

The PLA review (10.1007/s10311-023-01564-8) has reference-list chunks under its "Conclusion" section (pages 21–26), because bibliography detection missed them. They show up in some keyword results. They never hurt a golden question, but they waste candidate slots. Fix in the parser when a paper is next re-ingested.
